There's a spot on the west coast of Tasmania where a sign tells you the air blowing in off the ocean is among the cleanest measured anywhere on the planet — nothing between you and South America but open sea. We stood there breathing it in like idiots, and it genuinely felt different.

That's Tasmania in a sentence. It doesn't perform for you. It just quietly turns out to be one of the most beautiful places we've ever driven through.

## The roads are gloriously empty

After the traffic of the mainland, Tasmania feels almost secret. You can drive for long stretches and pass nobody — just paddocks, forest, the occasional wallaby making a poor decision at the roadside (slow down at dawn and dusk, seriously).

We based our loop loosely around Hobart, Freycinet, Cradle Mountain and the wild west, and never once wished we'd rushed it.

## Freycinet and the colour of the water

Wineglass Bay gets the postcards, and the lookout climb is worth it, but the whole Freycinet peninsula is the draw. The water is a colour that doesn't look real in photos and somehow looks even less real in person — impossibly clear, impossibly turquoise, ringed by pink granite.

We did the walk down to the beach, had it almost to ourselves, and ate a very ordinary sandwich that tasted incredible for reasons entirely to do with the view.

> Tasmania doesn't hand you its best moments at the lookouts. It hands them to you on the drives in between, when you're not ready with the camera.

## Cradle Mountain in the mist

We hiked around Dove Lake beneath Cradle Mountain on a morning thick with mist, the peaks appearing and disappearing like the mountain couldn't decide whether to show up. Wombats grazed the buttongrass, completely unbothered by us. It felt ancient and a little bit magic.

The weather here turns on a coin. We had four seasons before lunch. Layers, always.

## A few honest tips

- **Book Cradle Mountain and Freycinet ahead** in peak season — the good spots fill and the shuttle systems have limits.
- **Fuel up often.** Towns thin out fast on the west coast; don't gamble on the next station being open.
- **Give the west more time than you think.** Strahan, the rainforest, the Gordon River — the wild side rewarded us most, and we'd underbudgeted it.

## The honest cost

Getting there (and getting a car onto the island, if you bring one via the ferry) is the real expense; once you're on the road, Tasmania rewards a slow, self-catered trip. We spent less here than a comparable week on the mainland, mostly because there's little to spend money *on* except fuel, park passes and the odd very good meal.

We came for a long weekend's worth of expectations and left wishing we'd cleared a fortnight. Tasmania is a slow road. Take it slowly.
