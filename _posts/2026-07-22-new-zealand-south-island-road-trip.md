We picked up the campervan in Christchurch on a grey Tuesday, and for the first hour neither of us said much — partly the nerves of driving a house on wheels, mostly the realisation that we had two whole weeks and an entire island to get lost in.

By the end, we'd covered the South Island top to bottom, slept beside lakes we couldn't pronounce, and learned that in New Zealand the road between two places is almost always better than either place itself.

## The route, roughly

We looped: Christchurch → Lake Tekapo → Wānaka → Queenstown → Milford Sound → back up the West Coast → Arthur's Pass → Christchurch. On paper it's a lot of driving. In practice, you want the driving. Every pass opens onto something that stops the conversation dead.

If we did it again we'd add days, not destinations. Two nights minimum per stop. The temptation to keep moving is real, and it's a mistake.

## Tekapo will ruin other night skies for you

Lake Tekapo sits inside an International Dark Sky Reserve, and on a clear night the Milky Way doesn't so much appear as *arrive*. We stood in a freezing car park at midnight, necks craned, not saying anything, for the better part of an hour.

Bring every layer you own. The cold is worth it.

> We stopped counting mountain passes somewhere after Wānaka. You stop photographing them, too — not because they get less beautiful, but because you finally start just looking.

## Milford Sound in the rain

Everyone chases a blue-sky Milford Sound. We got rain, and honestly? The rain is the show. When it pours, hundreds of temporary waterfalls appear down the cliff faces, and the whole fiord turns silver and moody and enormous.

The drive in — through the Homer Tunnel and down into the valley — is as spectacular as the boat cruise. Fuel up before you go; there's nothing out there but scenery.

## What nobody tells you about campervan life

- **Freedom camping has rules.** Use the apps, respect the "self-contained" signs, and never assume a pretty pull-off is legal. Fines are steep and the locals are (fairly) protective.
- **Showers become a strategy.** Holiday parks, recreation centres, the occasional lake. You'll plan your week around them and feel oddly proud of it.
- **The weather changes everything, twice a day.** Check the passes. Arthur's Pass and the road to Milford can close fast.

## The honest cost

New Zealand is not a cheap country, and the campervan is the single biggest line item — but it's also your accommodation, so the maths works out better than it first looks. Fuel was our second surprise; the distances are long. We saved most by cooking in the van and only eating out every few days.

Would we do two weeks in a campervan again? In a heartbeat. Just with more nights per stop, and even fewer photos.
