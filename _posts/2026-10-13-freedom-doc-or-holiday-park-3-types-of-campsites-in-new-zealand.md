Planning a campervan trip around New Zealand? Googling "where do I even park this thing tonight?" Am I right?

When we planned our NZ road trip, I thought the hardest part would be driving a giant van along winding mountain roads. But boy, I was wrong. The real question every single day was: *where are we sleeping tonight?* And the answer changes everything, from whether you get a hot shower to whether your heater still works at 2am.

---

## The 3 types at a glance

Here's the quick comparison, based on what we saw on our trip (prices in NZD per night):

|  | Freedom camp | DOC campsite | Holiday park |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Price** | Free | \~$10 to $20 | \~$30 to $60 |
| **Toilet** | No | Yes | Yes, plus hot showers |
| **Power** | No | No | Yes, powered sites |
| **Dump station** | No | No | Usually |
| **Kitchen** | No | No | Yes, plus laundry |
| **Vibe** | Scenic & remote | Close to nature | Can be crowded |
| **Best for** | Self-contained vans only | Budget nights in nature | Charging & resetting |

---

## Each type, up close

### Type 1: Freedom camps

The dream, right? Park somewhere stunning, wake up to mountains, pay nothing.

- **Pros:** Free. Scenic and remote.
- **Cons:** No toilet, no dump site, no power, no kitchen. It's just you and nature.

**Note:** These are only for **self-contained vehicles** (vans with their own toilet and waste tanks). Look out for the signs, because some areas are marked as freedom camping restricted.

### Type 2: DOC campsites (Department of Conservation)

Run by NZ's Department of Conservation, these are the sweet spot if you're on a budget (being the broke-ish travellers that we are, we appreciated these a lot).

- **Pros:** Cheap (around $10 to $20 a night). Toilets available. Close to nature.
- **Cons:** No power, no dump site, no kitchen.

**Note:** Bring cash or book ahead online.

### Type 3: Commercial campsites (Holiday Parks)

The "hotel" of campsites. You pay more, but you get your life back together.

- **Pros:** Power hookup to charge your campervan's battery. Toilets and hot showers. Kitchen and laundry. Some even have playgrounds.
- **Cons:** The most expensive (anywhere from $30 up to $60 a night). Can be crowded.

**Note:** Best for charging and resetting.

---

## Tips we wish we knew earlier

- **Stay at a powered campsite every 1 to 2 days.** It charges your batteries and keeps the heater running, which is super important in winter. Trust me, you don't want to find out your battery is flat on a freezing night.
- **Make your first night a commercial campsite near the pickup location.** Especially if it's your first time doing the campervan thing. If anything goes wrong with the van, help is close by.
- **Always check the self-contained rules before parking overnight.** Read the signs, every time.

### The app that saved us: THL

## So, which one should you pick?

Well, a mix of all three. Each one has its own job:

→ If you crave views and your van is self-contained, go **Freedom camp**.

→ If you want nature on a budget with a toilet nearby, go **DOC**.

→ If your battery is low, your laundry is piling up and you need a hot shower, go **Holiday park**.

> **Freedom camps for the views. DOC for the budget. Holiday parks for the reset.**

My final verdict: plan a powered holiday park every 1 to 2 nights (non-negotiable in winter), and fill the nights in between with DOC and freedom camps. That way you get the best of NZ without waking up to a dead battery and a cold van.
