Have you been scrolling TikTok and seeing story after story of people getting deported from Jeju? Have you been overthinking your whole trip because of it? Because same.

Before our Jeju trip back in January, my FYP was full of deportation stories. And being the overthinker that I am, I did what any anxious Indonesian traveller would do: I prepared a folder like I was applying for a Schengen visa (spoiler: we barely needed any of it).

---

## An opening note

First off, to give you context: we're an Indonesian family based in Singapore (a red dot country 🇸🇬), travelling on Indonesian passports. Jeju has its own visa-free entry scheme, separate from mainland Korea. So as long as you fly **direct** into Jeju (no transit through Seoul or Busan), you can come in without a visa. We took a direct flight from Changi, so on paper, we were all good.

But "visa-free" doesn't mean "no questions asked". From what I've seen, most of the deportation stories come down to immigration not being convinced you're there as a genuine tourist. So our goal wasn't getting a visa. It was looking like the most boring, well-planned tourists ever.

Quick disclaimer: rules can change, so please double-check the latest requirements with the Korean embassy or your airline before you fly. I'm only sharing what worked for us :)

---

## The "just in case" folder: docs we brought

Here's everything we packed, printed and saved offline (because you never know if the Wi-Fi will cooperate at the counter):

- **Round-trip flight ticket.** Proof that you're actually going home. If you only bring one thing, I'd say it's this one.
- **Hotel / Airbnb booking.** Every night of the stay, with the address clearly visible.
- **Transport booking.** So it's clear you've planned how you're getting around, not "figuring it out later".
- **Simple itinerary.** Nothing fancy, just a day-by-day of where we're going (the UX designer in me made it a little too neat, I'll admit).
- **Bank statement.** To show you can afford the trip and aren't planning to overstay.
- **Cash, around 300,000 KRW.** Good to show if asked, and handy once you're out exploring anyway.

---

## So, what did they actually ask?

Well, this is the plot twist.

| Checkpoint | What happened |
| --- | --- |
| Changi check-in | A few questions: our accommodation booking details and how much KRW cash we were bringing |
| Jeju immigration | Didn't ask ANYTHING. Just a quick look at the passport here and there, and that's it |

Yep. After all that prep, the only real questions came from the airline counter in Singapore. By the time we landed, it was a stamp and "Annyeong, Jeju!!!" ✨

So was the folder a waste? Not at all. That folder is the reason I could walk up to the counter calm, instead of rehearsing answers in my head like it's a job interview. And since the questions came at check-in, it's worth being ready from the moment you're at Changi, not just when you land.

---

## Key takeaway

> **Visa-free ≠ zero checks.** Better over-prepared than stressed at the counter.

**What I learned:**

- **Prep for check-in, not just immigration.** That's where we actually got asked questions.
- **Know your numbers.** Where you're staying, how many nights, how much cash you're bringing.
- **One folder, printed and offline.** So you're not scrolling through your inbox in front of the officer.

Would I bring the whole folder again next time? Yes, 100%. It costs you 15 minutes of printing and saves you a lot of nerves.
