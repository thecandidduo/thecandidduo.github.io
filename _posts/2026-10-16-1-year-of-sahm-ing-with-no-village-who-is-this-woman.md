A year ago, if you told me I'd one day take a 3-hour flight alone with my son, I would've laughed. Nervously. Then probably panicked a little.

First off, to give you context: we're an Indonesian family living in Singapore (a red dot country 🇸🇬). That means no grandparents a short drive away, no helper, no babysitter on speed dial. **No village.** Just me, my husband, and our tiny boss who runs the whole household.

So after a full year of SAHM-ing (that's stay-at-home mum, for the non-mama friends reading this), I sat down and asked myself: who is this woman now? Turns out, she's quite different from the one who started.

---

## 💪 I feel like I can tackle (almost) anything

This is the biggest shift. When you don't have anyone to pass the baton to, you just... figure it out. There's no "let me ask Mama to help" or "the helper can handle that". It's you, every single time.

And somewhere along the way, "I can't do this" quietly turned into "okay, how do I do this?"

Being the designer that I am, I'd describe it like this: I stopped seeing every new situation as a scary problem and started seeing it as a flow I just haven't mapped yet. Once you've survived enough of them, the fear of the unknown gets a lot smaller.

> **No village didn't make me weaker. It forced me to build my own confidence from scratch.**

---

## ✈️ From scared to be alone with him, to a solo 3-hour flight

Let's be real, this one's a little embarrassing to admit. When I first started out as a SAHM, I was **scared of being left alone with my own kid.**

Not because I didn't love him (obviously). It was because I just wasn't used to it. I kept thinking: how am I supposed to get through a whole day without any help at all? No maid, no babysitter, no family. What if something happens? What if I can't cope?

But boy, was I wrong about myself.

Fast forward one year, and I can take a **3-hour flight alone with my son, no problem.** Just the two of us, the snacks, the bags and a whole lot of confidence that past me definitely did not have.

**What I learned:**

- **The fear was mostly about the unknown.** Once it became my everyday, it stopped being scary.
- **You don't need to feel ready to start.** I wasn't. I got ready by doing.
- **Your kid is learning to trust you too.** We kind of figured each other out together.

---

## 🧘‍♀️ More patience... but also less? (still figuring this one out)

This one's confusing, and honestly, I'm not sure I've cracked it.

On some days, I surprise myself. I can stay calm through things that would've totally frazzled me a year ago. On other days, my patience runs out way faster than I'd like to admit.

My own take? When you're the only one on duty, all day, every day, your patience becomes a **muscle and a battery at the same time.** It gets stronger with practice, but it also drains faster when there's no one to tap you out.

→ With a full tank, I'm the most patient version of myself I've ever been. → On an empty one, not so much.

So maybe it's not that I have more or less patience. **Maybe I just feel the swings more now.**

---

## ☕ Me-time? Never heard of her

Okay, here's the not-so-glamorous part. **I have basically zero me-time.**

My son is very, very attached to me right now. And it's not even that my husband won't help. I'm literally begging him to take over for a bit, and he's more than willing. The problem is my son. **He's the one who doesn't want it.** Mama or nothing, apparently.

It's sweet (and I know this phase won't last forever), but man, it can be draining. Even a quick shower or a hot coffee feels like a luxury some days.

If there's one thing I'd tell my past self, it's this: **don't wait for me-time to be handed to you.** It might not come in big, neat blocks for a while, so I'm learning to take the small pockets wherever I can find them.

---

## ✨ So, who is this woman now?

One year in, I'm braver than I thought I could be. I'm more tired than I'd like to be. And I'm still very much a work in progress.

A village makes motherhood easier. **No village makes you find out what you're made of.**

I'm only sharing my own experience here. I'm in no way, shape or form saying doing it alone is better (if you have a village, hold on to them tight!). But if you're a mama just starting out without one, and you're scared like I was: you'll be okay. Slowly, then suddenly, you'll realise you've been okay all along.
