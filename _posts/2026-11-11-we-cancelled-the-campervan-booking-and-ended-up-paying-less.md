## An opening note

Have you ever booked something because it was "the cheaper option", only to spend the next few nights doom-scrolling reviews and second-guessing yourself? Yeah. That was us.

Back in October 2023, we planned our very first campervan road trip in New Zealand. Six days on the road, starting and ending at Christchurch Airport, in a 4 berth camper (because more space is always better, am I right?).

Being the budget-conscious couple that we are, we went straight for the option that looked cheapest. Seemed logical. But boy, was this story about to take a turn.

---

## 🚐 The original plan: Cheapa Campa

Our first booking was the **Cheapa Campa 4 Berth**, pick-up on Tue 24 Oct 2023 and drop-off on Mon 30 Oct 2023, both at their Christchurch Airport branch.

The damage? **NZD 2,519.05**, or about **NZD 359.86 per day** on average.

Not exactly pocket change, but we went with it because it was supposed to be the budget pick. Booking done, itinerary started, life was good.

---

## 🤔 So, why did we cancel?

After booking, I fell down the review rabbit hole (as one does). From what we read, Cheapa Campa vans are basically **older campervans from another brand under the same parent company, THL, rebranded** for the budget market.

Now, that's not a bad thing on its own. But this was our **first time ever doing a campervan trip**, in a country we'd never driven around before. The thought of an older van acting up somewhere between two mountains with no signal? Not the vibe.

So we decided to play it safe: cancel, and look for a newer unit from another THL brand instead.

---

## No shade to Cheapa Campa, though

Just to be clear, this is not a dig at Cheapa Campa. Our dates were probably just a weird exception. On **most dates, Cheapa Campa is waaay cheaper** than the other THL brands, so they clearly have their own market.

- **Who it suits:** people who want a budget-friendly camper with the same facilities, just in an older van.
- **The reviews:** there were honestly LOTS of good ones.
- **Why we still skipped it:** purely because it was our first NZ road trip, and we didn't want to gamble on it.

If you've done a campervan trip before and you're comfortable with a slightly older ride, I'd say give them a look.

---

## 😮 The plot twist: Maui was cheaper?!

A week after cancelling Cheapa Campa, we booked the **Maui Beach 4 Berth** for the exact same dates and the same Christchurch Airport branch.

And guess what. Maui, the newer and supposedly fancier option, came up to **NZD 2,354.64**, or about **NZD 336.38 per day**.

That's **NZD 164.41 less** than our original booking. We literally paid less for a newer van. I'm still a little bit shook.

> **Sometimes the "budget" option isn't the budget option. Always compare the actual quotes.**

---

## What I learned

- **Compare the actual quotes.** The "budget" brand isn't always the cheapest on your dates.
- **Check prices again before you commit.** Ours shifted within a week, and in our favour.
- **First trip? Pay for peace of mind.** A newer van meant one less thing to stress about on unfamiliar roads.

## My final verdict

For a first-timer, I'd lean towards a newer van every time, especially when the price gap is small (or, in our lucky case, negative). For seasoned road trippers chasing the lowest price, the budget brands can absolutely make sense.

I'm only sharing what happened to us, so please do your own comparing for your dates.
